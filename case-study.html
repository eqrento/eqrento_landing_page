The challenge

Machines don’t stop working at 5 pm on Friday.

A rental contract says five days, eight hours a day. What actually happens on site is another story: a Saturday shift to catch up, a second crew in the evening, a machine kept running “just for an hour”.

Before EQrento, BAC Polska worked across several separate systems. Telemetry data lived in one place, contracts in another, and someone had to compare them by hand. Extra hours were noticed by chance, if at all.

How it works

GPS hours checked against every contract, automatically.

  1. 01 GPS logs engine hours Telematics on every machine reports when it actually works.
  2. 02 Compared with the contract Agreed days, daily hour limit, rental period.
  3. 03 Every overuse flagged Weekends, extra hours. Under 30 min/day is ignored.
  4. 04 Office decides Bill it, waive it, or bring it up with the customer.

Control, not autopilot

Nothing lands on an invoice without a human decision.

EQrento doesn’t add charges by itself. BAC’s office sees every flagged case and decides. A loyal customer running two hours over? Maybe let it go. A machine working every Saturday on a weekday contract? That’s a conversation, and now there’s data behind it.

  • Full visibility of work outside the contract, per machine and per customer
  • Built-in 30-minute daily tolerance, so loading and transport never trigger a flag
  • Hard numbers for customer conversations, even when the hours aren’t billed

What changed

From paper and WhatsApp to one source of truth.

Before

  • Everything on paper: contracts, notes, checklists
  • Handover and return photos scattered across WhatsApp
  • Paper handover protocols
  • GPS hours compared with contracts by hand
  • No automation between departments

With EQrento

  • Every rental in one system, from offer to invoice
  • Photos attached to each handover and return
  • Digital handover protocols
  • GPS hours checked against every contract automatically
  • Invoices calculated automatically and sent to the ERP on set days

A typical case: the customer reports a machine ready for pickup on Friday. Logistics collects it on Monday. In between, GPS shows it working all weekend. Before, nobody would have known. Now the office sees it on the next invoice run and decides what to do.
